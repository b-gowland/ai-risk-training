# Strategy: will anyone come, and what would make them?

> **Partly superseded on 27 November 2026.** The diagnosis below still
> holds. The engine in §4 (voice challenge, paid panel studies, ad seed)
> is on hold: the next 90 days go to At Home through people who run

Written 18 November 2026, revised 19 November (global audience) and 25
November (GitHub restored). Covers the free sites: the scenarios, the
reference library and the Governance Baseline. It replaces the earlier
growth plan. What to do, in order, is in [`PLAN.md`](PLAN.md); the product
critique is in [`PRODUCT_REVIEW.md`](PRODUCT_REVIEW.md). Sources are at the
end.

## 1. Where things stand

- **Traffic is close to zero.** 14 visitors in the 28 days to 18 October,
  most of them on the Baseline. Repeated LinkedIn posts drew little
  response.
- **Several properties for one person:** scenarios, library and Baseline.
- **The free product is good.** Three separate reviews agree the scenarios,
  artefacts and cards are better crafted than most paid compliance
  training. Quality isn't the problem. Being found is.

## 2. What the evidence says spreads

| Tool | Scale | Format | The hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human or Not (AI21, 2023) | Over 2 million players, 15 million chats | 2 minutes: chat, then guess human or bot | A headline number: 32% couldn't tell |
| Gandalf (Lakera) | Hundreds of thousands; became the company's main marketing channel | Levels, a few minutes each, a clear win | A challenge you can beat, and brag about |
| Bad News (Cambridge) | Tens of thousands in the study period; BBC, CNN, Reuters | 15 minutes, with a score | Published research: "21% less likely to believe fake news" |
| MIT Detect Fakes | 16,500+ people, 240,000 guesses | Real or fake? Pick one | Research numbers, repeated by the press |
| Starling Bank "Safe Phrases" (UK, 2024) | National press across UK media and CNN | One survey statistic plus one action | "28% think they've been targeted; agree a safe phrase" |

The pattern:

1. **Under two minutes to the first result.** Our scenarios take seven to
   ten.
2. **A result you can share** ("I got 2 out of 5"). Ours deliberately has
   none: "nothing scored".
3. **A number the press can print**, ideally from a credible study. We have
   none yet.
4. **One action people can take today** (agree a safe phrase). Ours has
   this already: the tell and "one thing this week".

## 3. Challenges to the earlier plan

**3.1 It was a product plan when the problem is demand.** Five phases and
about thirty items, most of them polish. None of the polish changes whether
a stranger hears about the site.

**3.2 Ads into a ten-minute scenario are expensive.** Australian Facebook
clicks cost roughly A$1 to A$3 (other English-speaking markets are similar
or cheaper). If a third of people who click start the scenario and half of
them finish, a completion costs A$6 to A$18. A$1,300 buys perhaps 70 to 200
completions: too few to publish a finding.

**3.3 The findings can be bought more cheaply and credibly.** Prolific, a
research participant panel, has a minimum pay of £6 an hour (£9
recommended) plus a 42.8% fee for companies. A three-minute task costs
about 65p a person. Participants are screened by country, age and
employment, in about 40 countries. The press trusts a study more than a
website's own visitors.

**3.4 The earlier plan was more Australian than the ambition.** The aim is
the best free AI risk training in the world. The content is close to ready: only 19
Australia-specific references in 4 of the 9 scenarios, mostly "where to
report" and "your rights" lines. The stories work anywhere.

- **Local help, global story.** Keep each story universal. Move "where to
  report" and "your rights" into a short block chosen by country (Scamwatch
  in Australia, the FTC in the US, the UK's national fraud reporting
  service, and so on), picked in the browser from its own language and time
  zone. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- **Findings from several countries**, each with its own figure, so each
  country's press has a local number.
- **English first, translation where it's cheap.** A 60-second challenge is
  a few hundred words, so it can go into Spanish, Portuguese, French,
  German and Hindi early. Full scenarios follow where a language shows
  demand.

**3.5 Free AI literacy is crowded.** The US Department of Labor launched a
free course by text in March 2026; Google and Goodwill aim at 200,000
learners; Elements of AI passed a million; Canada has a national
initiative. We can't win as another course. We can win as **practice**:
decisions with consequences, which none of those offer. A flight simulator
for AI risk.

**3.6 "Kill the site" saves nothing financially.** Hosting costs nothing.
What costs is time and ad money. So the decision is whether to keep
investing. If not, freeze: the sites stay up, keep their search and
AI-assistant value, and cost nothing.

**3.7 Risks**
- **Ad review.** Meta and Google screen ads about scams, money and voice
  cloning hard. Expect appeals; word ads around protection, not money.
- **Voice clips need consent.** Consenting volunteers and a cloning service
  whose terms require consent, or a research dataset licensed for public
  use. Never a real person without consent.

## 4. The engine

```
  BUY A FINDING        →   LET EVERYONE TAKE THE SAME TEST   →   SPREAD IT
  (research panel)          (a 60-second challenge, with a       (press, partners,
                             shareable score, then the            small paid seed,
                             full scenario)                       share links)
         │                              │                                │
         └── home door: public, several countries, voice clone ── reach, press, partners
         └── work door: workers who use AI, several countries ── teams, workplaces
```

### 4.1 Two quick challenges, in front of the scenarios

**"Is that really Mum?": the voice challenge (home door, worldwide)**
- Five short voice clips. Which ones are clones? Under 60 seconds.
- The twist is the lesson: most people do badly, and research says they
  will. The result screen says: "You got 2 of 5. So does almost everyone.
  You can't tell by listening, so don't try. Hang up and call back on a
  number you already have." Then "Now try the real call" opens the
  scenario.
- A shareable score, with WhatsApp as a first-class share button. The share
  link carries `?ref=share`, so Plausible counts how many people each player
  brings, with nothing new collected.
- Voices in several accents, so it feels local wherever it's played.
- "Nothing scored" stays true for the scenarios. A score in the challenge is
  a deliberate exception.

**"Real or invented?": the citation challenge (work door, worldwide)**
- Five references from an AI-drafted report. Which exist? About 90 seconds.
- Same twist: confident fakes are hard to spot, so check the source
  yourself. Then it opens "Confident and Wrong".

### 4.2 Buy the findings

Two Prolific studies; about £1,000 to £1,500 in total. Check current prices
first.

| Study | Who | What they do | The headline it could produce |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voice clones | About 800 people across five countries (US, UK, Australia, Canada, South Africa or India), grouped by age | The voice challenge, plus "what would you do if this call came?" | "X% couldn't pick a cloned voice, and Y% of over-60s would send the money", per country |
| AI at work | About 800 employees who use AI: half in the EU, half in the UK and US | The citation challenge, plus "would you paste this client document into a free AI tool?" | "X% of workers couldn't spot an invented legal citation", per region |

Publish the method and the numbers whichever way they come out. Add the
randomised study (roughly £3,000) if gate 1 passes:
does playing a scenario improve what people do a week later, compared with
reading a tip sheet?

### 4.3 Spread it

- **Press, by country:** one release with each country's figure, to consumer
  and tech reporters in each study country and the wire services (AP,
  Reuters, AAP, PA). For the work study: HR and workplace trade press.
- **Global partners first:** GASA, the Global Anti-Scam Alliance (members
  in 90+ countries, including Google, Meta, Microsoft and OpenAI). Then
  national bodies: AARP's Fraud Watch Network (US), Get Safe Online and Age
  UK, Scamwatch and Good Things Australia (a warm lead), and their
  counterparts elsewhere. Libraries and digital-skills programs. For the
  work door: Go1's content marketplace (check terms) and OER directories.
- **Small paid seed, about A$800:** Meta for the voice challenge in four to
  six English-speaking countries at once, to compare cost per completed
  challenge (watch for bot clicks in cheap-click countries). LinkedIn for
  the citation challenge, aimed at people who run workplace learning. Measure in Plausible by `utm_`
  tag and country. No pixels.
- **Calendar hooks:** "agree a family safe word before Christmas"
  (December); Safer Internet Day (9 February 2027, about 180 countries);
  International Fraud Awareness Week (November); national weeks such as Scams Awareness Week in
  Australia.

## 5. Gates

| Gate | When | Keep going if | Otherwise |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Play and share | 3 weeks after the challenge launches, after about A$500 of seed ads | Under A$1.50 per completed challenge; at least 30% go on to the scenario; every 10 players bring at least 2 more | Rework the hook once; if it fails again, stop paid traffic |
| 2. Pickup | 6 weeks after the findings go out | At least one press story or partner placement; at least 5 new sites linking in | Stop outreach; freeze the `.org` |

A freeze means: sites stay up at no cost, no more ad money or outreach
time, and a short note on the About page. If a gate passes, the rest of
`PLAN.md` continues.
